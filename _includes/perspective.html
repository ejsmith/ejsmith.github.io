AI, judgement and the build loop

AI is an amplifier. It doesn't care what it amplifies.

Give an experienced engineer with good instincts an AI agent and you get something close to a small team. Give the same agent to someone who doesn't understand the business, the customer or the real cost of complexity, and you get the wrong thing: built faster, explained more confidently, and in more lines of code than anyone will ever review.

That's why I believe three skills deserve equal weight when we hire, promote and mentor engineers, and AI makes all of them matter more, not less:

Business sense
Weighing what something is worth against what it will cost to build, run and support.
Common sense
Noticing when something doesn't add up, even when the AI sounds confident.
Technical depth
Telling good output from merely plausible output.

How I put it to work

Anyone can get an AI agent to produce code. Getting the right thing, reliably and at scale, takes a system. I build complete loops where agents plan, build, test, review and fix their own work against automated checks, so we can take on far bigger projects and ship far more without letting quality or consistency slip.

Guardrails: AI gets no more access than the person using it, every change is traceable, and architecture and trade-offs stay with people. "The AI wrote it" is never an answer.
